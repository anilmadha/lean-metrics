How this is calculated

BMR uses the Mifflin–St Jeor equation, generally regarded as the most accurate resting-metabolism estimate for most adults: 10×weight(kg) + 6.25×height(cm) − 5×age + 5 (men) or −161 (women).

TDEE multiplies BMR by an activity factor (1.2–1.9) to account for daily movement and exercise.

Calorie target adjusts TDEE up or down based on your goal, with a safety floor so it never drops suggested intake below roughly 1.1× your BMR (or 1,200/1,500 kcal), and estimates weekly weight change using the standard ≈7,700 kcal-per-kg energy equivalence.

Protein follows the International Society of Sports Nutrition's position stand — 1.4–2.0 g/kg for general training, and up to 2.3–3.1 g/kg of lean mass in a deficit to protect muscle. If you enter body fat %, the higher lean-mass-based range is used for fat-loss goals.

Fat targets 22–30% of calories, with a floor of about 0.6 g/kg bodyweight to protect hormone health, per the ISSN position stand on diets and body composition and USDA dietary fat guidance (20–35% AMDR).

Carbs fill the remaining calories. A note appears if that falls under ~130 g/day, the amount the brain needs from glucose per Institute of Medicine guidance.

Water uses a common rule of thumb of about 33 ml per kg of bodyweight, plus an activity bump (0.1–1.1 L) for your selected activity level. Hotter climates, illness, or breastfeeding raise this further.

Fiber uses the widely cited 14 g per 1,000 kcal rule of thumb (derived from the ~25g/2,000 kcal and ~38g/2,900 kcal Adequate Intake values from the Institute of Medicine).

Sodium and added sugar are shown as ceilings, not targets: 2,300 mg/day sodium (FDA) and 25 g/day for women or 36 g/day for men on added sugar (American Heart Association) — lower if you have high blood pressure or your doctor advises otherwise. The app doesn't log these from your food yet, so treat them as things to keep an eye on rather than numbers it's tracking for you.

Food-log macros come from a small built-in reference list of common foods (typical USDA-style values) or whatever you enter manually — they're estimates, not lab measurements.

These are population-level formulas, not a medical assessment. Individual metabolism varies by 5–15% or more. If you have a health condition, are pregnant, or are under 18, talk to a doctor or registered dietitian before changing your diet.
